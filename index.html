Philosophical Stance A

Dualism

The mind (non-physical soul, consciousness) and the body (physical matter, brain, organs) are two separate entities that interact with one another.

Key Question: If your body dies, does your mind or soul continue to exist independently?
Philosophical Stance B

Monism

The mind and physical body are aspects of the same unified reality. Thoughts and emotions are purely biological chemical and electrical processes of the physical brain.

Key Question: Are your thoughts simply electrical impulses fired across your brain's neurons?

Philosophical Antecedents: Rationalism vs. Empiricism

Key thinkers who shifted human understanding from mystical explanations to structured philosophical inquiry.

René Descartes (1596–1650)

French Philosopher & Mathematician
  • Interactive Dualism: Mind and body are separate, but interact through the pineal gland in the brain.
  • Rationalism: Knowledge comes from reasoning, logic, and innate ideas rather than sensory experience alone.
  • Famous Maxim: "Cogito, ergo sum" ("I think, therefore I am").
Impact on Psychology:

Framed the brain and nervous system as mechanisms subject to physical study.

John Locke (1632–1704)

English Philosopher
  • Empiricism: Knowledge originates exclusively from sensory experience and observable evidence.
  • Tabula Rasa: The human mind at birth is a "blank slate" written upon by life experiences.
  • Rejection of Innate Ideas: We are born without pre-programmed knowledge.
Impact on Psychology:

Laid the groundwork for scientific observation, experimentation, and behavioral learning theories.

Wilhelm Wundt & The Birth of Scientific Psychology (1879)

How psychology transitioned from philosophy to an independent laboratory science in Leipzig, Germany.

Wilhelm Wundt (1832–1920)

Known as the "Father of Experimental Psychology." In 1879, at the University of Leipzig (Germany), Wundt established the world's first psychological laboratory.

Key Method: Introspection

Self-observation where trained participants described their immediate conscious experiences (sensations, feelings) when exposed to sensory stimuli (e.g., a metronome ticking).

First School: Structuralism

Formalized by Wundt's student Edward Titchener, aiming to break down conscious mind into basic "structures" or components.

400 BCE: Ancient Greece — Socrates, Plato & Aristotle

Socrates and Plato argued that knowledge is innate and that the mind is separable from the body. Aristotle later proposed that knowledge grows from experiences stored in our memories.

Key Contribution: Posed the earliest structured questions regarding human nature, memory, and perception.
